Process

Identify, develop, adopt.

Most AI projects fail before or after the build, so we put a stage on each side of it.

Stage 1 of 3

Identify

Check readiness, map the work

We begin with an AI readiness assessment: are your processes stable, is your information reachable, does your team have room for change? Some businesses stop here with a list of basics to fix or a product to buy. If you are ready, we sit with the people who do the work, map each workflow, and mark every retype, wait and chase.

What we do
  • Assess readiness: process, data, tools, people
  • Map workflows as they really happen
  • Rank fixes by hours saved against effort to build
What you get
  • A plain answer: fix, buy or build
  • A marked-up map of your workflows
  • A fixed scope for the first build, if one is justified
Try the short readiness check
Stage 2 of 3

Develop

Build it around how you work

We write the software. It is shaped to your process and connected to the tools you already pay for. You see working software early, test it on real jobs, and tell us what is wrong while it is still cheap to change.

What we do
  • Design the build with the people who will use it
  • Develop and connect it to your existing systems
  • Test against your real cases, including the awkward ones
What you get
  • Working software you have already tried
  • Integrations with your current tools
  • Plain-language documentation
Stage 3 of 3

Adopt

Make the new way the normal way

This is the stage most projects skip, and the reason so many tools sit unused. We train your team on their real work, run the new process alongside the old one until it is trusted, and fix friction as it appears.

What we do
  • Train each role on its own tasks
  • Stay close through the first weeks of live use
  • Measure usage and time saved, then tune
What you get
  • A team that uses the tool without being asked
  • Playbooks for new team members
  • A report on what the build is saving
Stage one

Three ways Identify can end.

Custom software is the right answer less often than agencies admit. You get whichever of these is true.

Outcome A

Fix the basics first

The process changes each time, or the information lives on paper and in inboxes. Automating that only makes the mess faster. We tell you what to tidy up.

Outcome B

Buy, do not build

A product already on the market does most of what you need. We point you to it.

Outcome C

Build

The process is stable, the volume is there, and nothing off the shelf fits how you work. We scope the first build.
