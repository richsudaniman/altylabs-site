The problem

Where the time goes at the receiving desk

01

Five minutes of typing per case

Every slip is read and keyed into the lab software field by field, often from handwriting.

02

Problems found at the bench

A missing shade or an unclear tooth number is often noticed only when a technician picks up the case, and the due date does not move.

03

Afternoons on the phone

Each missing detail means a call to the dental office, time on hold, and a note on the case about who said what.

Inside the system

Take it apart, step by step.

Dark blocks are done by the system. The light block is your staff's approval. Switch to Before to see the same case done by hand.

1. Collect

Before

Slips in the box, PDFs in the inbox, portal notes in another tab, photos on someone's phone.

With the system

Watches the lab's inbox and a scan folder, with a phone photo upload at the receiving desk. Everything lands in one queue.

2. Read

Before

Someone reads each slip, handwriting included.

With the system

An AI vision model pulls out the doctor, practice, patient name or ID, tooth numbers, restoration type, material, shade, due date, special instructions and any attached files.

3. Check

Before

Problems surface when a technician starts the case.

With the system

Is the doctor a known customer? Is the tooth number valid? Does the shade make sense for the material? Can the due date be met with your turnaround times? Is anything required missing?

4. Flag

Before

A phone call to the office that eats someone's afternoon.

With the system

Drafts the message to the dental office, such as “Shade wasn't specified for #14, please confirm.” Your staff send it.

5. Approve

Before

About 5 minutes of typing per case.

With the system

Staff see the slip on one side and the filled-in case on the other, fix anything that is off and click approve. About 30 seconds.

6. Create

Before

Retyped into the lab software.

With the system

The case is created in your lab software through its API or an import file, or as a last resort by filling in the screen automatically.

Details

What it checks before a case is created

The questions get asked when the box arrives, not when a technician picks up the case.

Known customer

The doctor and practice are matched to an existing customer account.

Valid tooth numbers

Tooth numbers are checked for validity and against the restoration type.

Shade against material

The shade is checked against the shades available for that material.

Due date against turnaround

The requested date is checked against your turnaround times for that restoration and material.

Required fields

Anything your lab needs that is missing is flagged, with a drafted message to the office.

Attached files

Scans and photos sent with the prescription are attached to the case.

Pilot

How we set it up

It is tested on your own prescriptions before it touches your lab software.

  1. Sample your intake. We collect a few weeks of real slips, PDFs, portal notes and photos, including the messy ones.
  2. Encode your rules. Your customer list, materials, shades and turnaround times become the checks.
  3. Shadow mode. New cases are read and checked alongside your usual entry, so your staff can compare them field by field.
  4. Connect your software. Cases are created through your lab software's API or import file, or by filling in the screen where neither exists.
Fit

Is it right for your lab?

A good fit when

You receive a steady flow of cases from many offices, and someone spends a large part of the day typing them in.

Not a fit when

Nearly all of your cases already arrive digitally with complete, structured prescriptions.

Not sure? The readiness check gives a first answer in two minutes, and the ROI calculator shows what the hours are worth.

Which lab software does it work with?

It creates cases through your software's API or an import file where one exists. As a last resort it fills in the screen automatically, the same way your staff would.

Is anything saved without a person seeing it?

No. Every case is shown next to the original slip, and nothing is created in your software until someone approves it.

Does it contact dental offices on its own?

No. It drafts the message, such as asking for a missing shade, and your staff decide whether to send it.

Can it read handwritten slips?

Yes. Scanned and photographed slips are read by an AI vision model. Anything it is unsure of is highlighted for the person approving.

What about cases sent through iTero or 3Shape?

Notes from the scanner portal are read like any other prescription, and the attached files go with the case.
