The problem

Why research eats the office's week

01

Most of it is clerical

Deciding what to buy, then reading long registers full of mortgages and discharges that don't matter to the survey.

02

Records take days to arrive

Plans held by other firms are requested by email and chased by hand, so jobs sit waiting.

03

Quotes wait on research

Messy records mean more work, so proposals stall until someone has looked. Meanwhile the profession is short of people.

How it works

Research effort only goes into jobs you have won.

Minutes, before the job is won

Stage 1: Quick quote

  1. New job. Paste the inquiry email or call notes. The client, address, job type, purpose and deadline are filled in, with follow-up questions for anything missing.
  2. Repeat check. If you have surveyed this property or a neighbour before, the old job shows up immediately.
  3. Quick pull. The clerk buys only the subject register. The system reads it and raises flags: water boundaries, old township lots, unconverted land, easements.
  4. Proposal. The fee and proposal are drafted in your template, with each fee line explained. The surveyor approves it.
After the client accepts

Stage 2: Full research

  1. Pull list. One daily list of exactly what to buy in Teraview for all accepted jobs, minus anything already on file. About 5 to 10 minutes per job for the clerk.
  2. Reading. Every register, plan and instrument is read. What matters to a surveyor is kept, mortgages and deleted items are skipped, and newly referenced documents go back on the list.
  3. Prior surveys. Your archive is searched and the PSRI search terms are written for you.
  4. Records requests. Requests to other firms are drafted with follow-up reminders. Your staff send them.
  5. Brief and review. One screen per job shows the property and each neighbour side by side. The surveyor reviews only what is flagged or uncertain.
  6. Research package. On sign-off: the brief as a PDF, an organized document folder, an easements schedule, a field search list by boundary, and the registry fees to bill.

Pilot

How a pilot runs

You see it work on your own jobs before anything goes live, and the time saved is measured against your own numbers.

  1. Backtest on your completed jobs. We run 20 to 30 of your finished jobs through the system. For each one, we check how many of the documents your surveyor actually used it found.
  2. Baseline from day one. Whoever does research logs office hours per job, so the savings are measured, not estimated.
  3. Shadow mode. On new inquiries, the system runs alongside your usual process and you compare the results.
  4. Go live when it earns it. Only once it is finding what your surveyors use and research hours are down against your baseline.
Fit

Is it right for your firm?

A good fit when

Boundary surveys, reference plans or real property reports are a steady part of your work, and research is a daily load on the office.

Not a fit when

Most of your work is construction layout or topographic surveys with little title research.

Not sure? The readiness check gives a first answer in two minutes, and the ROI calculator shows what the hours are worth.

Does the software decide where the boundary is?

No. It gathers and organizes evidence, with every line linked to its source. The licensed surveyor weighs the evidence and makes every determination.

Does it log into Teraview or OnLand for us?

No. The system never scripts Teraview or OnLand. It writes a pull list and your clerk does the buying, usually 5 to 10 minutes per job.

Where does our data live?

Your firm gets its own instance and data, hosted in a Canadian region. Your data is not used to train models, you can export it any time, and it is deleted when you leave.

Will it find our old surveys?

Your archive is indexed and searchable by PIN, address, township, lot and concession, and plan number. That powers the repeat-job check and the prior-survey search.

How do we know it works on our jobs?

Before anything goes live, we backtest it on 20 to 30 of your completed jobs and measure how many of the documents your surveyor actually used it found.
